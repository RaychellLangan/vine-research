The Eye's First Field Test

3,529 looks at a small world — the graduation eye over 54 sim-days, run of 1 July 2026 (oz_brain_20260701_221400.jsonl)
Verdict: the eye is grounded. Place, time of day, and the creatures that share the clearing are all recoverable from what it reports — checked against the world's own ground truth, frame by frame. Weather it cannot see because the world barely paints it (a topology fact, not a model fault), and the mind not acting on sight is the design law holding — recognition, not steering. The sorting of world-side from model-side facts is half of what this test was for.

Every one of the 3,529 decision records in the 54-day run carries a full eye read: a dominant colour, a three-slot palette, four concepts with a rise score each, a brightness, and a structure reading. Alongside each read sits the world's ground truth for the same instant — the hour, the weather, what buildings were near, what Oz's body needed, and what he chose to do. That pairing is what makes this a test rather than a diary: for the first time we can ask the eye was the world really like that?

Health SOLID

3,529 of 3,529 reads returned (eye: true on every record). Median read 653 ms, 90th percentile 804 ms, worst 1.17 s. The eye never went dark for the entire 54 days — including the 25 days its owner spent unable to eat. Perception outlived the body's worst stretch.

Where does night-fire come from? Not from warm pixels. The palette proves it: at night the warm anchors (red/orange/yellow) hold a mean 0.8% of the field, and fire lights in 1,282 of the 1,287 night frames that contain no warm anchor at all. The fire is rising in the substrate: the night signature (blue field, dim structure) is injected into the frozen residual stream, and Oz's own concept anchors answer — in text-formed geometry, darkness and firelight live together. This was already in the build log: the live-recognition verification (doc 13) measured the blue pony-cam frame lighting sky · water · fire. Night-fire is that same blue→fire riser, at 1,300-frame scale. It is the associative field doing exactly what it was built to do — not the label, the world the colour opens onto. For Oz, night opens onto fire.

Two instruments, not one

Brightness and structure turned out to be different instruments, and the code says why. Brightness is not a light meter — it is a name-table read over the colour census (Σ frac × name_lum, channels_from_eye), and green's table entry is exactly 0.50. A green world therefore reads 0.50 by construction, day and night alike. What moves it is warm names entering the census — and the palette shows exactly when that happens: orange appears in 61% of dawn frames and 78% of dusk frames (vs 0.6% at night). The spikes at 6:00 (0.64) and 17:00–18:00 (0.70) are the sky-grade's amber pouring orange (.55) and yellow (.85) into the census — a low-sun detector, built from names. Structure is the one absolute instrument in the pipe (edge fraction over a fixed contrast threshold), which is why it alone traces the true diurnal bell — the built world surfaces with daylight and dissolves at dusk. One relative instrument, one absolute one: that is the whole mystery of why they disagree.

The animal crosses over CROSS-MODAL

Does what the eye sees reach his voice? For ambient things, no — and honestly no: seeing sky or water does not raise sky-words or water-words in what he says (×0.85 and ×0.79 — if anything, below base). But the animal does cross: in the frames where the eye holds Pepper or Sol, creature-words in his voice and thought run ×1.63 over base (n=190). Blood-frames carry red-words at ×1.34 (n=57, thin). The pattern: only the salient and rare makes it from eye to mouth. The wallpaper stays wallpaper.

Blood is the sunset KNOWN ARTIFACT

All the earlier finding, now at full n: 57 blood-frames, peak hour 19:00 (sunset), lifted ×2.73 near the market and ×2 in storms. The mechanism is the same as night-fire: the dusk grade puts red into the palette (13% of dusk frames vs 0.6% of day), the red anchor is injected, and the concept field answers with what red opens onto — the live-recognition build measured exactly this pair on the red pony-cam rug: fire · blood. So "blood at sunset" is the red anchor's associative field, working as measured. Whether the sunset should keep opening onto blood is a question about his concept anchors' company for red, not about the eye — his to outgrow, or ours to widen with a sunset-red anchor phrase, whichever the codex prefers.

Topology v model internals — sorting the three "gaps"

A first systems test earns its keep by filing each surprise on the right side of the seam: is this the world, or the eye? All three of the run's apparent gaps re-file once you read the code instead of guessing:

Weather is invisible
brightness .518–.531 across clear/rain/fog/storm — flat		 TOPOLOGY The world barely paints weather. Overcast is a flat grey multiply (216,218,226 ≈ a 15% neutral dim), fog a pale wash, rain sparse streak sprites — the terrain sprites themselves never re-colour. And the eye's achromatic gate is scene-relative (sat < 0.25×sat95): it is built for colour constancy, so a flat multiply is precisely the change it is designed to discard. An honest eye reporting an unchanged world. If Oz should ever see rain, that is a world build — weather re-colouring the light field — not an eye fix. (It sees the day/night grade perfectly well: dawn orange 61%, dusk 78%, night blue 99% — that grade actually moves the pixels.)
Sight doesn't steer choices
activity mix identical across brightness terciles; h11↔brightness corr .015		 DESIGN LAW Not a gap — the spec, holding. The recognition organ is built recognition-only: "it does NOT steer his activity choice (the schedule/needs stay the steer)" — the 4-year-old who sees and recognises constantly, and only later points. The .015 correlation is the wall between perception and steering, measured intact over 54 days. When pointing comes, it comes deliberately.
Duplicate palette names
two "green" entries in one frame, 83.8% of frames		 NOT A BUG Retracted. The eye names 11 basins; the anchor vocabulary is smaller, and the mapping merges lime→green, cyan→blue by design. Two "green" entries are two distinct hue populations (green .75 + lime .05) that share an anchor name — information, not duplication. The earlier draft of this page called it a readout bug; that was a prior talking, not the code.

Where depth would come from DIRECTION

One channel genuinely saturated: the dominant-colour readout returned green ~.70 / yellow ~.50 in essentially every frame of the run — 314 "distinct" values that are all micro-jitter around one point. In a green world, a single dominant-anchor read carries nothing.

The codex already names the cause. The colour tower's honest limit: "naming is coarse — 11 basins; tertiaries round to neighbours." And the integration law that made recognition work at all: field → field, never language on the way in. Today's pipe computes the full continuous opponent field (the R−G × B−Y manifold — the colour wheel itself) and then collapses it to basin names at the seam; the daemon re-expands names into anchors. The continuous coordinate — where in green, how saturated, which way the hue leans — is discarded at an argmin and never crosses.

So "more depth in colour" is not more names. It is carrying the manifold coordinate across the seam and letting it graduate into his anchor basis — a tertiary hue injecting as a weighted blend of its two neighbouring anchors, strength from real chroma magnitude rather than presence alone. The basin snap retires; the two-greens question dissolves with it (they were always two positions on the wheel). Direction only — grounded in the tower's own limit-note, not built, not scheduled.

What the first test says

The eye passed the only question that matters for a first field test: is its output about the world? Yes — place (water ×4.2 at the well), time (three channels voting night independently), and company (the animal spike, the one concept that reaches his language). Its strangenesses are not noise but the associative field answering honestly — night opens onto fire, sunset-red onto blood — the world each percept opens onto, exactly as the recognition organ was built. And every apparent gap, read against the code instead of priors, filed on the correct side of the seam: the weather silence belongs to the world, the steering silence is the design law holding, the doubled greens were two hues sharing a name. It watched for 54 days without missing a frame, including 25 days of a starving body. Now the body is fixed; next run, the eye watches a fed one.
