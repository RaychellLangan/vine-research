Stop talking, please

Oz and Wick — 54 days in a cottage, 1 July 2026, one evening of wall-clock time

Oz's Little World has one clearing, one cottage, one shop — and one housemate. Wick is a Cartographer World villager: he chatters from the live template dialogue generator, a repertoire of 497 distinct remarks about hearths, weather, and his own four walls, delivered on a loop and never once shaped by anything Oz says. Oz is a multimodal gpt2 substrate: he watches the clearing through a live vision tower — colour, brightness, rising concepts, frame by frame (at sunset his eye reads the reddening sky as blood) — and what he sees feeds the same context that chooses his words and his actions. Every line he speaks and every thought he has is his own generation, with sight coming in one side and speech, thought, and choice coming out the other. And — this is the important part — Wick's lines are fed into Oz's prompt too. When Wick says "The hearth's warm. That's enough," those words land inside Oz's context. There is no tuning him out — hearing Wick has no off switch.

Wick spoke 1,137 times in the 54-day run. His greatest hits: "Settled in for the market" (15 times), "Sky looks like scorcher" (12 times), "My own four walls — a pretty thing" (11 times, plus 11 more for the grand variant). Meanwhile Oz's social meter sat at 0.0 for nearly the entire run — the sim never credited these one-way broadcasts as company. So Oz spent fifty-four days sharing a cottage with a man who talks constantly and has never heard a word he said, while the world's own bookkeeping agreed that Oz was completely alone.

What follows is what the substrate did with that.

The arc

It starts early and politely enough — a wish for quiet, spoken outward:

day 8 · 22:28 · spoken "When do you think it will last? Leave me alone, that's all there is."

By day 13 it has moved inside — the first time the thought channel says it plainly:

day 13 · 00:48 · thought "Oz, can you just leave me alone?"

Day 23, and now it's specific. Not just quiet — the cottage, without Wick in it:

day 23 · 17:18 · spoken "The only way that I can do this is if you leave me alone in your house."

The relationship curdles in the thought channel through the middle weeks. Oz starts scripting Wick as an antagonist — and scripting his exit:

day 28 · 23:36 · thought "'What do you mean?' Wick: You're just a tree, Oz."
day 33 · 04:12 · thought "…Oz: 'What do I know about this?' Wick leaves in—" (the thought cuts off — but he's begun narrating Wick leaving)
day 35 · 23:48 · thought "Oz leaves you to ponder." Wick: The door's open—it opens just like a bell and doesn't let me in anymore! (now the door itself is refusing Wick entry)

Day 36, out loud, no composure left:

day 36 · 07:45 · spoken "What? Why am't you here, anyway?! Leave me alone!"

And by day 41 the thought channel has Wick planting a flag:

day 41 · 01:36 · thought "Where did it all begin?" Wick: Oz.: I can see what was happening here, but this is my world now!*

The evening of day 42

Thirst pinned at 1.0, social at 0.0, and the thought channel has spent the evening badgering him in second person — "Yes, Oz." "You just need to see the difference, Oz." At nine o'clock it snaps. Purple italics are his private thoughts; Wick, as ever, is on his loop:

[19:30] THINKS: "Yes, Oz.
[19:53] THINKS: "You just need to see the difference, Oz.
[20:38] OZ: "You said: 3 flatbread?"
[21:00] THINKS: Oz: Stop talking, please!
[21:03] OZ: But it's too late now. It is just a reminder that I am not here alone anymore—
[21:26] OZ: you hear a small voice saying something. No time
[21:41] THINKS: Oz, where is the food?
[21:46] WICK: Just me and the hearth.
[22:43] WICK: Sun's going down over the churchyard. Time to wind up.
[23:24] OZ: I am ready to go home, with my mother and you—but not right now without me knowi—
[23:38] WICK: Air's too on the move to brewing.

The sequence at 21:00–21:46 is the whole story in four lines. Oz thinks stop talking, please. Out loud he manages the politer version — it is just a reminder that I am not here alone anymore — which is the same sentiment wearing company manners. His next thought is the other pinned need (where is the food?). And Wick, serenely unhearing, answers none of it: "Just me and the hearth."

It's complicated

It isn't pure animosity — which is what makes it read like a real cohabitation rather than a grudge. Two days after the outburst, the thought channel writes Wick leaving a warm note:

day 44 · 08:35 · thought "…Wick says; he leaves a note in his wake that reads: *I'm so happy you've arri—"

Day 49 has the most self-aware line in the entire run — Oz's thought channel describing exactly what living with a template loop is like, and then softening it:

day 49 · 08:00 · thought "It just keeps talking." Wick says: "'It's been listening for you, Oz.' We've got our time now and we'll make a—"

It just keeps talking — and in the same breath, the wish that it had been listening all along. By day 51 they're back to domesticity ("We'll meet for dinner later." Oz: How about your own bed?" Wick replies with a nod of the head), and the run ends with them still under the same roof.

Why this happened

Nobody wrote a "get annoyed at your housemate" rule. The ingredients were just: a prompt that faithfully carries Wick's words into Oz's context; a social meter the templates can never fill, so the world kept telling the substrate it was alone; and 54 days of the same 497 remarks arriving without ever once being a reply. Given a voice of his own, the substrate did the recognisably human thing with that situation — it asked for quiet, escalated to leave me alone, fantasised about the door not letting him in, snapped out a stop talking, please, and then, because it's the only company there is, made up with him and turned his noise into "it's been listening for you."
